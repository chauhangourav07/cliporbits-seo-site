7 Red Flags That a YouTube Promotion Service Is Selling Bot Views

By Gourav Chauhan · Published October 12, 2026

Real YouTube promotion runs your video as an ad through Google Ads. Bot sellers use the same words (“real views”, “safe”, “organic”), so the sales page won't tell you which is which. These seven red flags will, and one check in your own YouTube Analytics settles it.

Key takeaways
  • Real promotion is advertising: views arrive over days, cost depends on the country, and no exact count is promised.
  • Guaranteed subscribers, instant delivery, monetization packages and password requests are the clearest red flags.
  • Ad views show up as YouTube advertising in YouTube Studio's traffic sources; bot views don't.
  • YouTube holds you responsible for promoters you hire, and fake engagement can get a channel removed.

The 7 red flags

1. A fixed number of views for a fixed price

Real ad costs depend on the countries you target, the format and how people respond to your video. A seller who offers “10,000 views for $X” without asking who you want to reach isn't buying those views in an ad auction.

2. Instant or overnight delivery

Every ad goes through a Google policy review before it runs (YouTube says Promote reviews typically take 1–2 days), and campaigns spend their budget over days. “Views start in minutes” is not how advertising works.

3. Guaranteed subscribers, likes or comments

No one can make a real person subscribe or comment. YouTube's fake engagement policy bans artificially inflating these numbers, including through third-party services.

4. “Monetization” or “4,000 watch hours” packages

YouTube doesn't count watch hours or subscribers from ad campaigns toward Partner Program eligibility. A package sold as monetization hours is either fake traffic or something YouTube won't count. Here's YouTube's rule.

5. They ask for your password or channel access

Running ads on a public video only needs its link. Nobody promoting your video needs your login. Never share it.

6. They never mention Google Ads, or can't explain the campaign

Vague phrases like “our promotion network”, “real users” or “social media blasts” are a warning. A real service can tell you which ad platform it uses, which countries it's targeting and what results its campaign recorded.

7. Reviews describe a spike, then a drop

Buyers of bot views often report the same pattern: the count jumps almost overnight, then falls back or stalls, and the new “viewers” never watch anything else. Views from a real campaign build over its run, and the people behind them behave like any other viewer.

The one check that settles it

In YouTube Studio, open Analytics and look at the traffic sources for the promoted video. Views from your video running as an ad appear as YouTube advertising. If a “promotion” shows up mostly as external, embedded or direct traffic instead, it wasn't advertising. While you're there, check how long those viewers watched and which countries they came from.

One price myth to drop: cheap doesn't automatically mean fake. Real ad views can cost under $0.002 in lower-cost countries; in one worldwide campaign of ours, a view cost about $0.00025. Where the views show up in your Analytics tells you more than the price does.

What a legitimate service looks like

  • It says plainly that it uses Google Ads, and asks who your audience is and where they live.
  • It doesn't promise an exact number of views or subscribers, and explains why.
  • It shows campaign results you can cross-check against your own YouTube Analytics, like this real campaign report.
  • It has a refund policy for before the campaign starts and a named person accountable for the work.

Why it matters: you're responsible

YouTube's fake engagement policy applies to what people you hire do on your behalf, and channels can be removed for it. Real advertising is a product YouTube sells, so it doesn't break the policy. FAQ

Can buying views get my channel banned?
Buying fake views can. YouTube's fake engagement policy covers services you hire, and channels can be removed. Real Google Ads promotion is advertising YouTube sells itself, and it doesn't break that policy.
How do I know if my views came from ads?
In YouTube Studio, open Analytics and check the traffic sources for the video. Views from your video running as an ad appear as YouTube advertising.
Are cheap views always fake?
No. Real ad views can be very cheap in lower-cost countries; in one worldwide campaign of ours, a view cost about $0.00025. Price alone proves nothing. How the views are bought, and where they show up in Analytics, does.
Is it safe to use YouTube Promote?
Yes. Promote is YouTube's own tool, and YouTube says views and subscribers from it are legitimate.
